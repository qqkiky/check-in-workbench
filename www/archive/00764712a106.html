    PCSK9 Targeted Therapies Market Analysis

    • PCSK9-targeted therapies represent an important class of lipid-lowering therapies (LLTs), primarily used in patients with familial hypercholesterolemia or established ASCVD who need additional LDL-C reduction despite receiving maximally tolerated statin therapy. 

    • Since their introduction in 2015, large-scale clinical studies have shown that these therapies can reduce LDL-C levels by approximately 60% and lower cardiovascular event risk by nearly 50% in secondary prevention settings. 

    • However, despite their strong clinical efficacy, real-world uptake has remained constrained by high treatment costs, insurance and administrative barriers, and prescriber reluctance.

    • REPATHA, developed by Amgen, was among the first PCSK9 inhibitors to enter the market, receiving FDA approval in August 2015 and EMA approval in July 2015 for patients with HeFH, HoFH, and clinical ASCVD requiring additional LDL-C lowering. 

    • PRALUENT, developed by Sanofi and Regeneron, followed shortly after, gaining FDA approval in July 2015 and EMA approval in September 2015 for adults with primary hypercholesterolemia, including HeFH and non-familial forms, as well as mixed dyslipidemia.

    • Both therapies subsequently expanded their indications following major cardiovascular outcomes trials. The FOURIER trial in 2017 provided evidence supporting REPATHA for cardiovascular risk reduction, while the ODYSSEY Outcomes trial in 2018 supported an expanded indication for PRALUENT to reduce the risk of myocardial infarction, stroke, and unstable angina requiring hospitalization.

    • The PCSK9 treatment landscape was further strengthened with the introduction of LEQVIO (inclisiran), which received FDA approval in 2021. Unlike conventional PCSK9 monoclonal antibodies, LEQVIO employs small interfering RNA (siRNA) technology to suppress hepatic PCSK9 production, offering a distinct approach to sustained LDL-C reduction.

    • Recently, in July 2026, the U.S. FDA has approved Merck's LIPFENDRA (20 mg tablets) as an adjunct to diet and exercise for lowering LDL-C in adults with hypercholesterolemia, including heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH), marking the first approval of an oral PCSK9 inhibitor and a significant breakthrough in the PCSK9 inhibitor landscape.

    • The pipeline is also expanding, with several emerging PCSK9-targeted candidates under development, including Recaticimab from Hengrui Medicine, VERVE-102 from Verve Therapeutics, AZD0780 from AstraZeneca, AQR-008 from Aqur Biosciences, and other novel approaches. 

    • Among these, Recaticimab (Hengrui Medicine) represents one of the notable next-generation PCSK9-targeted therapies being evaluated for hypercholesterolemia and cardiovascular risk reduction.

    PCSK9 Targeted Therapies Competitive Landscape

    Some of the PCSK9 inhibitors drugs under development include Recaticimab (Hengrui Medicine), VERVE 102 (Verve Therapeutics), AZD0780 (AstraZeneca), AQR-008 (Aqur Biosciences), LIB003 (lerodalcibep; LIB Therapeutics), CVI-LM001 (CVI Pharmaceuticals), and others.

    Hengrui Medicine's Recaticimab is a fully human IgG1 monoclonal antibody that targets proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9). By selectively binding to PCSK9, it prevents PCSK9 from interacting with the low-density lipoprotein receptor (LDLR), thereby reducing PCSK9-mediated LDLR degradation. This increases the availability of LDLRs on the cell surface and promotes the clearance of LDL cholesterol (LDL-C) from the bloodstream. Its commercialization introduces an additional therapeutic option for patients requiring LDL-C reduction.

    AstraZeneca's AZD0780 is an investigational oral small-molecule PCSK9 inhibitor designed to address elevated LDL-C levels in patients who do not achieve adequate control with statin therapy alone. AstraZeneca is assessing AZD0780 as both a standalone treatment and as part of fixed-dose combinations with other therapies across its cardiovascular portfolio. The candidate is currently undergoing Phase III clinical development.

    Aqur Biosciences' AQR-008 employs a differentiated mechanism of action by targeting the EGF-A domain of the LDL receptor rather than directly inhibiting PCSK9. By preventing PCSK9 from binding to LDLR, AQR-008 is designed to protect the receptor while potentially preserving PCSK9's other biological functions, which continue to be investigated. This receptor-focused strategy highlights the evolving PCSK9 inhibitor landscape, with next-generation therapies pursuing alternative mechanisms that could offer greater selectivity, improved safety profiles, and enhanced treatment convenience.

    The anticipated launch of these emerging therapies are poised to transform the PCSK9 inhibitors market landscape in the coming years. As these cutting-edge therapies continue to mature and gain regulatory approval, they are expected to reshape the PCSK9 inhibitors market landscape, offering new standards of care and unlocking opportunities for medical innovation and economic growth.

    Recent Developments in the PCSK9 Targeted Therapies Market

    • In August 2026, LIB Therapeutics, Inc. announced that the FDA has approved an autoinjector for LEROCHOL (lerodalcibep-liga) Injection 300 mg/1.2 mL, a third-generation PCSK9 inhibitor designed to deliver robust, sustained efficacy through convenient once-monthly, single-injection self-administration, potentially supporting long-term treatment adherence. The FDA also approved an update to LEROCHOL's prescribing information to reflect its indication for reducing the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) in adults through LDL-C lowering, either with statins or with monoclonal antibody PCSK9 inhibitors used as an adjunct to statin therapy.

    • In July 2026, Merck announced that the FDA has approved LIPFENDRA (enlicitide) tablets, 20 mg, as an adjunct to diet and exercise for lowering low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) in adults with hypercholesterolemia, including those with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH). LIPFENDRA, a novel macrocyclic peptide, is the first FDA-approved oral PCSK9 inhibitor demonstrated to reduce LDL-C, commonly referred to as "bad cholesterol."

    • In July 2026, Evogene Ltd. and ELEO Inc. announced a strategic scientific partnership focused on the discovery and development of next-generation oral small-molecule inhibitors targeting the PCSK9 pathway for the treatment of hyperlipidemia and cardiovascular diseases.
